Sight reading

Music appears on a grand staff, you play it on a MIDI keyboard, and it keeps score. A note you get wrong turns red and stays red, so afterwards you can see exactly where you fell down.

Play

  • appSight reading

    Set the difficulty, pick a mode, and read. Chords, or one note at a time — scales, a melody, broken chords, with leaps and chromatics if you want them hard. Or load a piece of your own as MusicXML and it scores that the same way. Practice mode moves on whatever you play; strict mode will not let you past a chord, or a measure, until you get it right; timed mode puts a metronome behind you.

  • app… with no piano attached

    The same game with the computer’s letter keys standing in for a keyboard. For trying it out away from the instrument.

Checks

  • 0MIDI check

    Does this browser see your keyboard, and how fast? Run this first if the game is not hearing you.

  • 1Render check

    Hard-coded music holding all the awkward engraving cases side by side: ledger lines, accidentals, stems both ways, seconds, dots, beams. For judging whether the notation looks right.

  • 1Glyph check

    Which font on this machine draws the clefs and accidentals, and how well.

  • 2Sample piece

    A deliberately awkward eight measures — two hands in different rhythms, a tie across a barline, three kinds of rest, two voices, a clef change. Load it under “a piece of your own” to see the importer work.

On a computer, use Chrome or Edge. On an iPad, no ordinary browser will do — Safari has never supported Web MIDI and Apple makes every browser there use Safari’s engine — so use the free MIDIWeb Browser app, which brings its own MIDI plumbing. Nothing you load or play leaves the machine — your pieces are kept in the browser, not on a server.
Running it from a copy on disk instead? Double-click start.cmd rather than opening the files directly: a browser will only hand out MIDI over HTTPS or from localhost.
